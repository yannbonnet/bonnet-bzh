The subject of this opinion no longer exists: the transatlantic negotiation was
suspended and then abandoned. That is precisely what gives it its archival value, and
there is no point looking for a present-day relevance it does not have.

What is worth noting is that the question outlived its framework. The opinion voiced
concern in 2014 about the conditions under which Europeans' personal data could flow to
the United States. That question was not settled by a trade treaty but by a succession
of adequacy decisions and legal challenges: Safe Harbor invalidated, Privacy Shield
adopted and then invalidated in turn, a third framework adopted since. The opinion had
identified the right point of friction; it had been wrong about the instrument through
which it would surface.

One thing fell entirely outside the field of vision in 2014: the extraterritorial reach
of US law over service providers, formalised four years later. That became the central
argument in the French debate on trusted cloud computing, and nothing in this opinion
hinted at it.
